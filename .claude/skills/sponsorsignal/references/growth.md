# Getting people here, and getting them to stay

Read `SKILL.md` first.

## The one-line pitch

Lead with the pain, not the product:

> Only about 127,000 of the UK's 5 million employers can sponsor a work visa.
> Every application to the rest is wasted. Here is the list, searchable and
> updated daily.

That framing works everywhere: search, social, the newsletter, the meta
description. It states a real problem, gives a number, and offers the fix.

## Search — the channel that compounds

The only free channel that keeps paying after the work is done. It is slow.
Expect three to six months before it matters, and do it anyway.

- The landing pages exist because "companies that sponsor visas in Manchester"
  is what people actually type. Keep generating them from real data.
- Each page must carry genuine content — real employers, real counts. Thin
  pages that only link elsewhere get treated as doorway pages and demoted.
- Submit `sitemap.xml` to Google Search Console. Nothing is indexed quickly
  without it. Only the user can do this.
- Watch which queries land in Search Console and build pages for the ones that
  already get impressions. Let real demand pick the next pages, not guesswork.
- The change log is naturally good for search: "new UK visa sponsors, March
  2026" is a query with no good answer today, and we generate that data anyway.

## Communities — fast, one-shot, easy to waste

r/ukvisa, r/UKimmigration, r/immigration, plus Facebook and WhatsApp groups
for specific nationalities moving to the UK.

- Read each community's self-promotion rules first. Many allow tools only in
  specific threads or with mod approval. Getting banned costs the channel
  permanently.
- Give the value in the post itself. A post that answers the question and
  happens to link the tool outperforms a post that is only a link.
- Answer questions in the thread afterwards. That is where the trust is built.
- Drafts live in `launch/posts.md`. **Never post on the user's behalf.**
  Write the draft, hand it over, let them post it.

Do not launch until email capture works and "Added recently" shows real
results. Attention arrives once, and a dead form wastes it.

## The newsletter — the retention loop

Search brings people once. The newsletter is what brings them back.

One email a week: the newly licensed employers, grouped by industry, plus
anything notable such as a sector suddenly growing. `data/digest.md` is
generated for exactly this.

- Keep it short and genuinely useful. A list someone scans in thirty seconds
  and finds one lead in beats a long essay.
- Removals matter as much as additions. "This employer lost its licence" saves
  someone a wasted application, and that is the kind of thing people forward.
- Same writing rules as the site: see `references/copy.md`.
- Automate the sending only after the content is proven manually. A weekly send
  that nobody opens is not worth automating.

## Making the site itself do the work

- **Shareable filtered links.** Already built. "Here are the tech sponsors in
  Leeds" is a link someone pastes into a group chat, and that is free reach.
- **Answer the question fast.** Someone landing from search should see relevant
  employers immediately, not a homepage they must re-search from.
- **Ask for the email at the right moment**, after the search has proved useful
  rather than before. Never a popup over the results.
- **Say when the data was updated.** Freshness is the reason to come back, so
  make it visible.

## What to stop doing if it is not working

Check honestly every few weeks. Cut a channel that has had a fair run and
produced nothing, and say so plainly rather than quietly continuing.
